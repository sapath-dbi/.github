## About DBI

The **Data & Bioinformatics Innovation Unit (DBI)** is part of Genetics and Molecular Pathology at **SA Pathology, South Australia**.

We develop and apply computational methods, bioinformatics tools and data-driven approaches to advance genomic medicine, molecular diagnostics and translational biomedical research.

Our work brings together genomics, transcriptomics, computational biology, statistical modelling and emerging analytical technologies to address complex biological and clinical questions.

We are committed to rigorous, reproducible and reusable research outputs, including open-source software, analytical workflows and resources for the wider scientific community.

## Research Areas

- **Computational Genomics:** Variant interpretation, structural variation and genomic breakpoint analysis.
- **Transcriptomics & RNA Biology:** RNA sequencing, alternative splicing and gene expression analysis.
- **Bioinformatics & Software Development:** Computational tools, analytical methods and reproducible pipelines.
- **Single-cell & Advanced Sequencing:** Single-cell sequencing, UMI-based analysis and emerging sequencing technologies.
- **Molecular Diagnostics:** Integrated genomic analysis and translational bioinformatics.
- **Computational & Precision Medicine:** Statistical modelling, machine learning and clinical research applications.

## Publications & Reproducible Research

We aim to connect scientific publications with the computational resources supporting them.

Our public repositories provide research software, analysis scripts, documentation, reproducible workflows and example outputs wherever available.

We support transparent, reproducible and responsible computational research.

## Collaboration

We welcome collaboration with researchers, clinicians, bioinformaticians and software developers.

For software questions, bug reports and technical discussions, please use the Issues section of the relevant repository.

## Open Science & Responsible Research

Public resources are shared subject to their respective licences and institutional requirements.

Please do not upload patient-identifiable information, confidential clinical data or restricted institutional information.

Research software is not a substitute for validated clinical diagnostic procedures.
